# 2. Intro Section

### Heading

**Your Delivery Operation Shouldn't Depend on Calls, Chats, and Spreadsheets.**

When restaurants manage deliveries manually, dispatchers often have to coordinate orders through phone calls, WhatsApp messages, paper notes, and separate tracking tools.

That becomes increasingly difficult as order volume grows.

MhStudio brings the delivery workflow into one connected system.

From the moment an order becomes ready to the moment it reaches the customer, your team can see what is happening and take action when needed.

### Three Core Benefits

**Dispatch**

Get ready orders assigned to the right rider.

**Track**

See delivery progress and rider activity.

**Analyze**

Understand delivery speed, rider performance, and operational costs.

# 3. The Problem With Manual Delivery Management

### Heading

**More Orders Mean More Delivery Complexity.**

A manual workflow often looks like:

`Order Ready`

↓

`Call Rider`

↓

`Send Address`

↓

`Wait for Confirmation`

↓

`Track Through Phone`

↓

`Customer Calls Restaurant`

↓

`Call Rider Again`

This becomes difficult when multiple deliveries are active.

* Riders not knowing which order to pick up
* Orders waiting too long for assignment
* Manual rider coordination
* No central view of active deliveries
* Customers calling for updates

# 7. Rider Assignment

### Heading

**Assign the Right Rider to the Right Delivery.**

Dispatchers can manually assign orders to riders.

Example:

**Order #1057**

Customer: Ahmed
Zone: Hayatabad
Order Value: PKR 2,350

Available riders:

* Ali — 1.2 km away
* Hamza — 2.4 km away
* Bilal — 4.1 km away

**Assign Ali**

The system then updates:

`Unassigned → Assigned`

and the rider receives the delivery task.

# 11. Delivery Acceptance & Rejection

### Heading

**Know When a Rider Accepts—or Can't Take—an Order.**

When a rider receives an assignment, they can:

**Accept**

or

**Reject**

If rejecting, the system can require a reason.

Examples:

* Too far
* Vehicle issue
* Already overloaded
* Customer/address issue
* Other operational reason

The dispatcher can then reassign the order.

### Workflow

`Assigned`

↓

`Rejected`

↓

`Reassignment Queue`

↓

`New Rider`

This prevents rejected deliveries from becoming invisible.

# 37. FAQ

### Do I need my own riders?

No. You can operate with your own fleet, delivery partners, or a hybrid model depending on your requirements and available integrations.

### Is the rider app included?

A rider-facing application can be included/configured as part of a delivery management implementation depending on the selected package and requirements.

### Can I manually assign riders?

Yes.

### Can riders accept or reject deliveries?

Yes, with configurable rejection reasons where required.

### Can I automatically assign riders?

Yes, automatic or suggested assignment can be configured based on the restaurant's operational rules.

### Can I see riders on a map?

Live rider tracking can be supported when the rider application and location services provide the required data.

### Can customers track their delivery?

Customer-facing tracking can be implemented where the required tracking infrastructure is available.

### Can I use delivery partners?

Yes, supported delivery-partner integrations/workflows can be connected where technically available.

### Can I use both my riders and delivery partners?

Yes. A hybrid model can use both.

### Can I manage delivery zones?

Yes. Zones can have configurable delivery availability, fees, and other rules.

### Can I manage COD?

Yes. Cash collection can be recorded against the relevant rider and order.

### Can riders see the entire admin panel?

No. Rider access should be restricted to the delivery functions they need.

### Can delivery connect with my POS?

Yes. Delivery orders can flow through the same central order/POS workflow.

### Can delivery connect with my kitchen?

Yes. Delivery orders can enter the KDS and move into dispatch after preparation.

# Visual Design Direction

The page should feel like a **professional logistics/operations product**, while still visually belonging to a restaurant brand.

Use:

* Large delivery map
* Rider location markers
* Route visualization
* Dispatch queue
* Rider cards
* Mobile rider-app screens
* Delivery status timeline
* Zone map
* Performance charts
* COD cards
* Delivery KPI cards

Avoid:

* Generic food-delivery stock photos as the main visual
* Only showing a scooter/rider
* Treating the page as a simple "delivery service"
* Overly futuristic map graphics
* Fake-looking live GPS
* Excessive gradients
* Generic SaaS illustrations

The central visual message should be:

**Restaurant → Kitchen → Dispatch → Rider → Customer**

with the admin panel controlling the entire process.
